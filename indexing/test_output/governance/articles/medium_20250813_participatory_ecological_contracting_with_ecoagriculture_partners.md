[EcoAgriculture Partners](https://ecoagriculture.org) is one of those rare organizations operating in the world today that truly understands the dynamic and holistic connections between farms, farmers and landscapes. Their work on spatial modeling of participatory planning scenarios is brilliant and important, and we believe it is a first step towards the type of ecological and social data visualization that is needed to create a fully participatory regenerative agreement framework to anchor Regen Network ecological contracts between land stewards and downstream businesses, governments, and NGOs.

The following is a vision of a future pilot that Ecoag Partners and Regen Network are in the process of designing and finalizing. It’s written in the present tense to bring us there, but this is a scenario that is yet to happen. Both organizations will need to continue to build awareness and fundraise to make this vision a reality. It is perhaps the most important set of tools for the next twenty years of human civilizations: a toolset to shift from an extractive and degenerative economy to an economy grounded in landscape-scale regeneration as a cornerstone of our new global economy.

# Imagine

You are in a small village in Ghana in a mud hut. It is cool and spacious in the noonday sun, and dark enough to clearly see the 3D rendering of the earth floating in the middle of the room. People are speaking both the lingua franca of international development and aid — English, and the local dialect. Excitement builds as we start to zoom in like a shooting star coming to earth at an impossible speed towards the place on earth, over the Atlantic Ocean and over verdant hills and scarred landscapes until we can see the hut. Then zooming back out again, the process begins.

As conversation flows about history and future, desires and aims and potential scenarios, the landscape being viewed changes. Forests grow up or are cut down, floods and fires or verdant beautiful agroforestry systems. We are working through the participatory design of a landscape. This process embodies wisdom — both that of traditional storytelling and in the cumulative depth of western scientific and engineering wizardry. We are able to model and understand the consequences: both positive and negative of our land-use choices. We can model in market scenarios and the impact of cash crop choices. Population growth and impact on endangered species are all gracefully conveyed in an intuitive visual language. Discussion changes from creative storytelling to intense and thoughtful decision making. Over the next week, this village will be making decisions that will affect many generations to come. Participants in the process include a chocolate company that sources organic cacao from the area, the national government and international aid agencies. We are all seeing the first pilot of participatory ecological contracting via the new partnership between Regen Network and EcoAgriculture Partners.

Agreements between stakeholders and the measurement and validation mythologies that will anchor these agreements range from development loans (micro and very large loans to private sector actors) with interest rates variable from 0% to 20% tied to ecological performance indicators, to direct product premiums, and a suite of ecosystem service payments to land stewards. The mixed financing stack constituting a fully regenerative landscape approach is all anchored in long term agreements to track and record ecological outcomes and place strong incentives on the stewardship of common resources, carbon sequestration and the increase in ecological health and resilience over time.

Each agreement — and the monitoring, reporting and verification methodology that is best suited to understanding and verifying the ecological claim at the base of the agreement — is time-stamped and logged on Regen Ledger. This, in turn, creates a map of the ecological agreements, making it possible to visualize progress on natural climate change solutions, agricultural resilience, and biodiversity.

Welcome to the regenerative economy.
